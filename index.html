The Universal Circular Recovery Hierarchy

EcoLith prioritizes resource recovery across organic biology and inorganic technical hardware according to EPA, ISO 14040, and Ellen MacArthur Foundation standards—maximizing economic and material utility before downcycling.

Tier 1

Source Reuse & Refurbishment

Culinary upcycling for food trimmings; optical inspection, ultrasonic degreasing, and direct refurbishment for mechanical bearings and fasteners.

Tier 2

Redistribution & Core Exchange

Surplus edible groceries routed to food rescue networks; automotive alternators, electric motors, and server hardware routed to remanufacturing core exchanges.

Tier 3

Bio & Component Recovery

Biochemical pectin and mushroom cultivation; hot-air IC desoldering from PCBs, lithium-ion cell sorting, and high-value modular salvage.

Tier 4

Biogas & Secondary Smelting

Anaerobic digesters generating renewable methane; closed-loop secondary smelting of clean aluminum, copper, and polymer extrusion pelletizing (95% energy saved).

Tier 5

Regeneration & Abatement

Aerobic composting for regenerative organic soil amendments; certified e-waste hazardous abatement and slag mineral stabilization.

Knowledge Base & AEO

Frequently Asked Questions

Authoritative answers to common questions about organic food recovery, inorganic e-waste, scrap metal inspection, and AI vision privacy.

What is EcoLith and how does it solve waste and resource recovery problems? +

EcoLith is an AI-powered circular resource management platform that converts waste into actionable, high-value recovery opportunities. Each year, billions of tons of municipal food scraps, decommissioned electronics (e-waste), scrap metals, and industrial hardware are dumped into landfills or scrap heaps.

EcoLith solves this by providing universal multimodal vision scanning. Users snap a photo of kitchen trimmings or industrial hardware, and EcoLith instantly classifies the material, inspects physical condition and wear, calculates reusability scores (0–100), and matches the item with verified circular diversion pathways—from component harvesting and bearing refurbishment to biochemical extraction and composting.

How does EcoLith inspect and classify inorganic industrial, mechanical, and electronic waste? +

EcoLith uses custom multimodal vision prompts and specialized system instructions tailored for technical and industrial hardware. When an inorganic item is scanned, the vision engine evaluates:

  • Material & Alloy Classification: Identifies FR-4 PCBs, 6061-T6 aluminum, high-tensile steel, copper windings, HDPE drums, or lithium-ion chemistries.
  • Condition Grading: Assigns standardized grades including new_surplus, reusable_intact, minor_wear_refurbishable, damaged_repairable, scrap_recyclable, or hazardous_damaged.
  • Physical Defect Analysis: Scans for oxidation/rust, thread pitch damage, scoring on bearing raceways, swollen capacitors, or PCB burn marks.
  • Hazard Warnings: Proactively warns against handling charged capacitors, swollen battery cells, or toxic chemical coatings.
How is the reusability score and mechanical/electrical condition calculated? +

The Reusability Score (0 to 100) is an objective condition index evaluated by analyzing observable physical wear against baseline engineering tolerances:

  • Score 80–100 (Direct Reuse / Surplus): Zero structural deformation or fatigue. Fasteners have crisp thread crests, bearings rotate without brinelling, and PCBs have intact solder pads.
  • Score 55–79 (Refurbishable / Salvageable): Surface patina, minor rust, or light debris that can be restored via ultrasonic degreasing, repacking grease, or wire-brushing.
  • Score 25–54 (Component Harvesting Only): The main assembly is broken or obsolete, but individual subcomponents (ICs, MOSFETs, transformers, copper windings) are intact and harvestable.
  • Score 0–24 (Scrap Material Recycling): Severe fracture, deep pitting, or thermal damage. Material should be routed to secondary foundries (EAF steel, aluminum remelters) or hydrometallurgical refiners.
What safety precautions are necessary when scanning and handling inorganic waste? +

Industrial and electronic waste often harbors hidden physical, chemical, or electrical hazards. Always observe standard workshop and lab protocols:

  • Electrical Safety: Disconnect all power supplies and discharge high-voltage electrolytic capacitors using an insulated discharge resistor before touch or disassembly.
  • Lithium Battery Handling: Insulate battery terminals with Kapton or vinyl electrical tape. Never crush, puncture, or short-circuit lithium-ion cells due to thermal runaway fire hazards.
  • Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Wear ANSI Z87-approved eye protection, cut-resistant EN388 gloves when handling sharp sheet metal or wire, and ESD wrist straps when handling sensitive semiconductors.
How are precious metals and critical minerals recovered from decommissioned electronics? +

Decommissioned server motherboards, telecommunications cards, and printed circuit boards contain concentrations of gold, silver, copper, and palladium up to 50 times higher than natural gold ore.

EcoLith promotes a two-stage cascade: first, intact microcontrollers and power MOSFETs are harvested using hot-air rework stations for maker or lab prototyping. Second, the depleted board substrate is sent to certified e-Stewards or R2-certified hydrometallurgical processors who dissolve and refine gold and copper using eco-friendly closed-loop leaching, reducing carbon footprints by over 80% compared to open-pit mining.

Can engineering polymers, 3D printing scrap, and industrial plastics be recycled circularly? +

Yes, when segregated by polymer resin. Unlike mixed consumer packaging, industrial and engineering plastics (such as HDPE chemical drums, ABS machine enclosures, and PLA/PETG 3D printing waste) have known, consistent molecular weights.

EcoLith checks for resin codes and chemical residues. Containers undergo triple-rinsing, granulation into 8–10mm flakes, and melt-filtration extrusion to produce high-tensile recycled plastic pellets suitable for blow-molding industrial pallets, drainage pipes, or 3D printer filament.

Can citrus peels and acidic rinds be composted or recovered? +

Yes. While citrus peels (orange, lemon, lime, grapefruit) should be kept away from delicate vermicompost worm bins because of limonene oils and natural acidity, they compost exceptionally well in standard aerobic compost piles when mixed with carbon-rich brown matter (dry leaves, straw, shredded cardboard).

At commercial and industrial scale, citrus rinds are the world’s leading source of pectin—a valuable commercial polysaccharide used in jams, confectioneries, and pharmaceutical capsules ($2.50–$4.50/kg market value).

How can spent coffee grounds and tea leaves be used circularly? +

Spent coffee grounds and tea leaves are nutrient-dense waste streams containing bioavailable nitrogen, lignin, and essential trace minerals.

  • Gourmet Mushroom Cultivation: Coffee grounds provide a sterile, pasteurized substrate for cultivating oyster and lion's mane mushrooms (2.5 kg grounds yields up to 1 kg fresh mushrooms).
  • Organic Soil Amendment: Mixed into garden beds, grounds slowly release nitrogen without significantly lowering soil pH.
  • Compost Activator: Provides an ideal green nitrogen source to accelerate decomposition in slow compost piles.
How does the Bring Your Own Key (BYOK) privacy model work? +

EcoLith ships with no platform-owned AI key. Every user supplies their own Google Gemini API key via the dedicated, hardened Key Vault page.

Your key is encrypted on the server with AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption using a unique 12-byte initialization vector and stored in an isolated database schema that is unreachable from client queries. The key is decrypted strictly in memory for the duration of a single scan and is never logged, placed in URLs, or viewable by system administrators.

How are environmental metrics and carbon savings calculated? +

Environmental metrics in EcoLith are calculated deterministically using scientific emission factors established by the EPA Waste Reduction Model (WARM) and the IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories.

When food scraps decompose in municipal landfills without oxygen, anaerobic bacteria generate methane (CH₄), a greenhouse gas with 28 to 36 times the global warming potential of CO₂. EcoLith models approximately 2.5 kg of CO₂e emissions prevented for every 1.0 kg of organic waste diverted into composting, anaerobic digesters, or reuse. For metals and polymers, secondary recycling saves up to 95% of embodied smelting energy.

Can spoiled dairy, liquid whey, or cooked oils be safely diverted? +

Yes, but through specialized industrial pathways. Liquid dairy and whey have an extraordinarily high Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD > 40,000 mg/L) and must never be dumped down municipal storm drains.

Instead, dairy liquids are prime candidates for anaerobic co-digestion facilities, where their high lactose and butterfat content yields exceptional biogas volumes. Used vegetable cooking oils are collected for refining into ASTM-certified biodiesel.

Can AI vision guarantee that materials are safe or food is edible? +

No. AI visual analysis is an informational decision-support tool, not an absolute safety guarantee.

For food items, computer vision cannot detect microscopic pathogens (like Salmonella or E. coli), internal toxins, or chemical spoilage. For inorganic hardware, vision cannot substitute for ultrasound non-destructive testing (NDT), dielectric breakdown testing, or metallurgical fatigue tests. Always exercise professional verification and safety protocols before food consumption or industrial component reuse.

Important Safety & Environmental Notice: AI visual analysis is an informational decision-support tool. It is neither a guarantee of food edibility nor a substitute for certified engineering, electrical, or hazardous materials inspection. Always disconnect power sources, wear proper PPE (gloves, eye protection), and verify material condition, hygiene, and local regulatory requirements before reuse, refurbishment, or redistribution.
