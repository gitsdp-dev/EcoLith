Circular Economy Framework

The Circular Food Recovery Hierarchy

EcoLith prioritizes resource recovery pathways according to the EPA and international circular economy standards, maximizing economic value before organic decomposition.

Tier 1

Source Reuse

Culinary upcycling of clean trimmings: peel chips, stocks, herbal infusions, and scrap vinegar ferments.

Tier 2

Redistribution

Surplus edible food routed to local food banks, shelters, and community networks prior to spoil dates.

Tier 3

Bio-Recovery

High-value extraction: citrus pectin, coffee-grown gourmet mushrooms, and potassium fertilizers.

Tier 4

Anaerobic Biogas

High-moisture organics and starches digested into renewable methane gas and nutrient-rich digestate.

Tier 5

Aerobic Compost

Controlled humification with brown carbon, generating regenerative organic soil amendments.

Knowledge Base & AEO

Frequently Asked Questions

Authoritative answers to common questions about food waste recovery, circular biotechnology, and AI vision privacy.

What is EcoLith and how does it solve food waste problems? +

EcoLith is an AI-powered food waste intelligence and circular resource management platform. Each year, more than 1.3 billion tonnes of food is sent to landfills globally, where it decomposes anaerobically into potent greenhouse gases.

EcoLith solves this by instantly analyzing photos of surplus ingredients or prep scraps, determining their organic composition, and matching them with verified diversion pathways—including biochemical pectin extraction, gourmet mushroom farming, anaerobic biogas digestion, and thermal composting.

Can citrus peels and acidic rinds be composted or recovered? +

Yes. While citrus peels (orange, lemon, lime, grapefruit) should be kept away from delicate vermicompost worm bins because of limonene oils and natural acidity, they compost exceptionally well in standard aerobic compost piles when mixed with carbon-rich brown matter (dry leaves, straw, shredded cardboard).

At commercial and industrial scale, citrus rinds are the world’s leading source of pectin—a valuable commercial polysaccharide used in jams, confectioneries, and pharmaceutical capsules ($2.50–$4.50/kg market value).

How can spent coffee grounds and tea leaves be used circularly? +

Spent coffee grounds and tea leaves are nutrient-dense waste streams containing bioavailable nitrogen, lignin, and essential trace minerals.

  • Gourmet Mushroom Cultivation: Coffee grounds provide a sterile, pasteurized substrate for cultivating oyster and lion's mane mushrooms (2.5 kg grounds yields up to 1 kg fresh mushrooms).
  • Organic Soil Amendment: Mixed into garden beds, grounds slowly release nitrogen without significantly lowering soil pH.
  • Compost Activator: Provides an ideal green nitrogen source to accelerate decomposition in slow compost piles.
How does the Bring Your Own Key (BYOK) privacy model work? +

EcoLith ships with no platform-owned AI key. Every user supplies their own Google Gemini API key via the dedicated, hardened Key Vault page.

Your key is encrypted on the server with AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption using a unique 12-byte initialization vector and stored in an isolated database schema that is unreachable from client queries. The key is decrypted strictly in memory for the duration of a single scan and is never logged, placed in URLs, or viewable by system administrators.

How are environmental metrics and carbon savings calculated? +

Environmental metrics in EcoLith are calculated deterministically using scientific emission factors established by the EPA Waste Reduction Model (WARM) and the IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories.

When food scraps decompose in municipal landfills without oxygen, anaerobic bacteria generate methane (CH₄), a greenhouse gas with 28 to 36 times the global warming potential of CO₂. EcoLith models approximately 2.5 kg of CO₂e emissions prevented for every 1.0 kg of organic waste diverted into composting, anaerobic digesters, or reuse.

What is the difference between food waste recycling and circular resource recovery? +

Traditional food recycling is typically a flat, single-stage process: collecting all organic scraps into one bin and shipping them to a commercial compost site or biodigester.

In contrast, circular resource recovery follows a cascade principle: extracting the highest possible utility and economic value from a resource before allowing it to break down into soil. For example, citrus peel is first harvested for food-grade zest or essential oils, its pulp is processed for commercial pectin, and only the exhausted residue is aerobically composted.

Can spoiled dairy, liquid whey, or cooked oils be safely diverted? +

Yes, but through specialized industrial pathways. Liquid dairy and whey have an extraordinarily high Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD > 40,000 mg/L) and must never be dumped down municipal storm drains.

Instead, dairy liquids are prime candidates for anaerobic co-digestion facilities, where their high lactose and butterfat content yields exceptional biogas volumes. Used vegetable cooking oils are collected for refining into ASTM-certified biodiesel.

Can AI vision guarantee that surplus food is completely safe to eat? +

No. AI visual analysis cannot replace certified culinary food safety inspections (such as HACCP or ServSafe protocols). Optical cameras cannot detect bacterial pathogens (such as Salmonella, Listeria, or E. coli), microbial toxins, or invisible spoilage.

EcoLith provides directional categorization and diversion ideas. Users must always verify temperature history, cleanliness, aroma, and local sanitary regulations before redistributing or consuming any surplus food.

Important Safety Disclaimer: AI visual analysis is an informational tool and is not a guarantee of food safety or edibility. Always verify food condition, refrigeration history, hygiene, and local health regulatory requirements before consumption or redistribution.
